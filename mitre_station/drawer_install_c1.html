Mitre Station · Cabinet 1

Cabinet 1 Drawer Install

Two bays, four drawers each — one deep at the bottom, three shallow above. 20″ side-mount ball-bearing slides, inset faces flush with the carcass front. Install bottom-up: every face above references the one below it, so accumulated error ends up under the top stretcher where nobody looks.

Why the faces are taller than the boxes

The box floats inside its face

Each shallow box is 4 11/16″ (4 1/2 sides plus a 5mm bottom screwed underneath) behind a 5 1/2″ face, and the deep box is 10 13/16″ behind an 11 7/8″ face. That surplus is deliberate: it absorbs carcass error and slide slop, and it lets all eight shallow boxes stay one height.

The deep box sits 1/4″ up inside its face so it clears the cabinet bottom panel by 5/16″. That is not cosmetic: a loaded 1/2″ drawer bottom deflects about 1/8″ at the centre, and screw heads on the underside eat more, so a box set at the 1/16″ face reveal would drag on the deck once the drawer is full. Reveals are cosmetic, box clearances are not, and the two get sized separately. The rest of the surplus divides out to 7/8″ between every other pair of boxes.

The rule it has to satisfy is simply box no taller than its own face — a face plus its reveal is the entire vertical budget that box gets, so a box taller than its face fouls its neighbour no matter where the other drawers sit. Everything below follows from that.

Two spare shallow boxes come out of this. They will not drop into Cabinet 2 as they are: that bay is 18 13/16″ interior against an 18 9/16″ box, which is 1/8″ per side where a side-mount slide needs 1/2″. New front/backs at 16 13/16″ and trimmed bottoms would get there.

One bay, drawn to scale. Solid blocks are the faces; the dashed outline inside each is the box behind it, bottom-aligned. Red ticks are the four layout lines — the bottom edge of each box, measured up from the top face of the cabinet bottom panel. Transfer these to a story stick.

01

Measure the carcass first

The box is 18 9/16″ wide in a 19 9/16″ bay. That is exactly 1/2″ per side, which is exactly what a side-mount slide needs — there is no tolerance in it. Check before you glue any boxes up, while you can still trim.

  • Both bay widths, at the front edge and again at the back. Both want 19 9/16″. If the divider sits off-centre, that bay's boxes get trimmed to suit — not shimmed.
  • Opening height: top face of the bottom panel to the underside of the top front stretcher. Should be 28 11/16″, the same as the divider.
  • Divider square to the bottom panel, or every slide on it runs out of parallel.

If the opening is off by more than about 1/16″, change the reveals rather than the faces — spread the difference across the five gaps and remark the stick.

02

Make the story stick

Do not measure these four heights eight times with a tape. Mark them once and copy them.

  • Rip a scrap to about 30″ × 2″ and square one end.
  • Stand the square end on the cabinet bottom, inside the bay, and mark where the top stretcher lands.
  • Transfer the four layout lines from the table below. Label each one.
  • Use the stick on all four vertical faces: left cabinet side, both faces of the divider, right cabinet side.
03

Layout lines

All heights measured up from the top face of the cabinet bottom panel. The key column is the box bottom edge — that is the line you mark on the cabinet.

Set each box so its bottom edge is level with its face's bottom edge — the surplus face sits at the top, behind the reveal. Then set the slide's cabinet member to whatever offset your slide's instructions give from that box bottom edge. On most ball-bearing side-mounts that offset is zero — the member's bottom edge sits on the line — but confirm it against your own hardware before you drill forty holes.

04

Set the depth: 3/4″ back from the front edge

Inset faces sit in the plane of the carcass front, and the face is 3/4″ plywood. So the box front has to land 3/4″ behind the front edge, which means the slide's cabinet member starts 3/4″ back too.

Depth section. A 20″ slide set back 3/4″ ends at 20 3/4″, well inside both the 23″ cabinet side and the 22 1/4″ divider.

05

Hang the boxes, bottom-up

  • Mount the bottom pair of cabinet members first and test-fit the deep box before you commit to the other seven. If something is wrong it is wrong seven more times.
  • Drawer members go on the box sides, front end flush with the box front. A 20″ slide on a 20″ box is flush at both ends.
  • Work up. Check each drawer runs full travel and closes flush before you mount the next one — reaching past three installed drawers to fix the fourth is miserable.
06

Hang the faces, bottom-up

  • Drill two clearance holes through each box front, 5/16″ — deliberately oversize, so the face can be nudged before the screws bite.
  • Face 1: shim 1/16″ off the cabinet bottom panel and 1/16″ at each side. Ripped hardboard strips or playing cards both work.
  • Hold it with double-sided tape, close the drawer, then drive two #8 × 1″ pan heads with washers from inside the box.
  • Faces 2–4: shim off the face below, not off a measurement. That is why this goes bottom-up.
  • Sight the reveal, adjust, then add two more screws.

Things that will bite

Screws meeting inside the divider

The divider is 3/4″ thick with a slide on each face. Two 1/2″ screws driven at the same spot will meet. Use #8 × 1/2″ pan heads and stagger them — holes 1, 3, 5 on one face, 2, 4, 6 on the other. The slide has more holes than you need.

Drawer 1's line is not its face line

Every other box sits level with its face's bottom edge; the deep one is 1/4″ higher, so its layout line is 5/16″ off the panel, not 1/16″. Rest the cabinet member on a 5/16″ offcut laid on the deck and screw it off.

Slides are handed

Eight pairs for this cabinet, and left and right members are not interchangeable. Sort them into pairs before you start rather than discovering it on drawer seven.

The deep drawer's load

It is 20″ deep and 10 5/16″ of interior — full of hand tools that is easily 60 lb. Check the slides are rated for it, and pilot every screw in the 1/2″ ply edge grain.

You are three parts short

The deep drawers still need one Btm Side and two Btm F/B cut — they are on the new sheet in cut_plans_remaining.html, row 1 and the first strip of row 3.

Face heights carry the stack

The four faces plus five 1/16″ reveals fill the opening exactly, so face height is what has to be accurate — the boxes have over an inch of room each. Cut the faces from one saw setting per size and check the stack dry before you hang any of them.
